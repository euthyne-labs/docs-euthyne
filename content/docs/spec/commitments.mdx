Receipts never carry content in clear. Each content field becomes a commitment: a salted SHA-256 hash.

## The scheme

Each device holds a 32-byte commitment key `K` in its OS keychain. `K` never leaves the machine.

```text
salt       = HMAC-SHA256(K, "euthyne/v1" || receipt_id || field)
commitment = SHA-256(salt || value)
```

- `||` is byte concatenation.
- `receipt_id` is the receipt's `id`.
- `field` is the committed field name, the same as the `name` in the Statement's `subject`.

The receipt's `salt_key` field names the commitment key ID and epoch, so a verifier knows which key a reveal must come from.

<Callout title="Draft">
  The exact byte encoding of the HMAC input and of each value is not final.
</Callout>

## Properties

- **Hiding.** Without the salt, a commitment says nothing about the value, even a short or guessable one.
- **Binding.** Given a salt and a value, anyone can recompute the commitment and compare.
- **One field at a time.** Each salt comes from its own HMAC input. Revealing one salt tells nothing about any other.
- **Nothing stored per receipt.** Salts are derived from `K` when needed.

## Revealing a value

```bash
euthyne reveal <receipt> <field>
```

This prints the salt and the value for one field of one receipt. To check a reveal:

1. Verify the receipt itself. See [the four checks](/docs/spec/checks).
2. Compute `SHA-256(salt || value)`.
3. Compare it with the digest of the matching `subject` entry.

## Losing the key

If a device is lost, its `K` is lost too. Old commitments can no longer be opened, but the receipts stay valid and verifiable. An organization may keep a copy of `K` in its own vault. Euthyne never holds it.

## What a commitment does not prove

A commitment binds the device to a value at signing time. It does not show that the hook received the true value from the harness. A revealed value is the value that was committed, nothing more.
